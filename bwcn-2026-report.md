## Executive Summary

Zambia's blockchain and Bitcoin ecosystem has grown from a handful of enthusiasts in 2021 into one of the most active and organised grassroots digital asset communities in Southern Africa. What started as informal meetups and online conversations has produced structured communities, university Bitcoin clubs, merchant adoption, developer programmes, and partnerships with leading global Bitcoin and blockchain organisations.

This report documents that journey. It covers the communities that built the ecosystem from the ground up, the service providers and tools making digital assets accessible to everyday Zambians, and the merchants and circular economies putting Bitcoin and crypto to practical use.

The picture that emerges is not one of speculation or hype. It's one of people solving real problems: how to save in a currency that holds its value, how to send money across borders without paying half of it in fees, how to access financial services without a bank account, and how to build technical skills in an economy that needs them.

## Part One: Grassroots Blockchain and Bitcoin Communities (2022–2026)

The communities below form the backbone of Zambia's digital asset ecosystem. Each one was built without institutional support, government backing, or significant external funding — by people who believed in the technology and were willing to put in the time to explain it to others.

### 1. Blockchain and Web3 Community Network (BWCN) — 2022

The largest grassroots initiative advancing blockchain education, awareness, and adoption across Zambia. Founded in 2022 by young advocates passionate about decentralisation, bringing together students, entrepreneurs, developers, and everyday users.

**Focus:** Blockchain education and literacy, Bitcoin advocacy, community building, developer support, practical adoption.

**Highlights:**
- Held 30+ physical events and 20+ online webinars (2022–2025)
- Educated 300+ individuals through blockchain education scholarship programmes
- Built a community of 1,000+ members
- Hosted Zambia's inaugural Women in Blockchain Conference
- Celebrated Bitcoin Pizza Day for three consecutive years
- Organised developer training on smart contracts and blockchain tooling

## Part Four: Observations and the Road Ahead

### What the Data Tells Us

Zambia's blockchain and Bitcoin ecosystem is real, active, and growing — and growing fast. It was not built by institutions or pushed by government policy. It was built by communities, educators, and individuals who saw Bitcoin and blockchain as tools worth understanding and using.

A few things stand out.

**Growth has come with a natural sorting process.** A number of communities and initiatives have died out over the past few cycles — expected, and frankly welcome. As the industry matures and becomes less speculative, communities built on risky, speculative tokens fell away with each bear market. What survived focused on education, developer initiatives, physical meetups, and circular economies, and stayed simple: on-ramps, off-ramps, spending and saving solutions. Nothing exotic, just useful.

**Zambia has been largely spared the wave of crypto scams** that have damaged trust elsewhere. That relatively clean track record makes the environment more conducive for serious, credible players.

**Merchant adoption is real but concentrated.** Most Bitcoin-accepting businesses are in Livingstone, given the tourism economy and Bitcoin VicFalls' work. Lusaka has fewer merchant options than its community size would suggest — growing Lusaka's circular economy is a clear 2026 opportunity.

**The university pipeline is the most exciting development.** Bitcoin Zambia and BitDevs Zambia's outreach programme, spanning 15 institutions and 300+ software development students, is producing the next generation of Bitcoin builders. The November 2026 Buildathon will be the most significant technical event in Zambia's Bitcoin history. Zambia has deep, underused developer talent and is genuinely positioned to become one of Africa's blockchain development hubs.

**Policy is the clear laggard.** Regulators have largely worked in the background, with no visible consultation with active users and builders — one of the biggest risks to current momentum.

> **Projection:** Based on current trajectory, active initiatives, tech products, and users in Zambia's blockchain space are projected to double by the end of 2027.

### What Needs to Happen Next

1. **More merchant adoption in Lusaka.** The capital needs more crypto-accepting businesses; community members should actively support local businesses in setting up Lightning wallets.
2. **Stronger coordination between communities.** A shared calendar, joint events, and coordinated outreach would multiply each organisation's impact.
3. **Regulatory clarity, driven by proactive engagement.** Zambia's financial regulators have not produced clear guidance on Bitcoin and digital assets. Engaging the Bank of Zambia and the Financial Intelligence Centre proactively — pushing for open consultation, not just top-down rulemaking — is important before restrictive regulation arrives.
4. **Documentation and storytelling.** Better documentation of events, outcomes, and impact stories will help attract global partners, funding, and attention.
5. **International visibility.** Zambia should be a more prominent voice in the Africa Bitcoin conversation — participating in the Africa Bitcoin Conference, engaging global Bitcoin media, and getting Zambian projects onto platforms like BTC Map and OpenSats.
6. **Position Zambia as a developer hub.** With strong developer talent, a clean reputation on scams, and a growing university pipeline, Zambia has a real opportunity to market itself deliberately as one of Africa's blockchain development hubs — not just wait for the reputation to arrive on its own.
